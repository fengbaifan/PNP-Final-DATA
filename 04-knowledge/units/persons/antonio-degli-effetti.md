## 内容

### 描述

**中文：** 第6章称 Antonio degli Effetti 为古物研究者和地方史家；原藏画已失，Haskell 说了解其趣味主要依赖他自编的目录，同时指出目录中的部分画作是否实际拥有曾受质疑，但有独立证据表明他以收藏者闻名。外国旅人眼中，他的 Studiolo 似乎只陈列小画、缩微画、宝石和珠宝；Haskell 说明这些陈列对 Antonio 本人意义远超这种观感，并将其描述为文学兴趣浓厚的人：他在画廊入口复制一幅寓意人生的图画，并称其所藏多数作品旨在阐释该寓意的教训。Haskell 提到当时归于 Cebes 的《The Table》，脚注则纠正 Pinax 并非 Cebes 所作、可能出自公元1世纪；他对 Antonio 将财富本身作为题材的判断保留为作者解释。

**English:** Chapter 6 identifies Antonio degli Effetti as an antiquarian and local historian. His pictures have disappeared; Haskell says knowledge of his taste depends chiefly on the catalogue he compiled, while noting doubts about whether he actually owned some listed pictures and independent evidence that he was known as a collector. To foreign travellers, his Studiolo might appear to contain little more than small paintings, miniatures, precious stones, and jewels; Haskell says its significance to Antonio was much greater. He describes Antonio as a man of letters: Antonio reproduced an allegorical picture of human life at his gallery entrance, and Haskell says most pictures he did own were intended to illustrate its lessons. The chapter mentions a dialogue called The Table, then attributed to Cebes; its footnote corrects that the Pinax was by another author and probably dates from the first century CE. Haskell's interpretation that Antonio delighted in wealth as a theme in its own right remains attributed to the author.
