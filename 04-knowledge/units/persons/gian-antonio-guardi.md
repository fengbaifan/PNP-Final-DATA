## 内容

### 描述

**中文：** 第10章称 Gian Antonio Guardi 在1730年前至1745年间为 Marshal Schulenburg 工作并领取月薪。哈斯克尔说 Schulenburg 主要把他视作有用的临摹者，而非独立画家；Guardi 只在1737年受托创作过一组自己的历史画，此后主要临摹威尼斯经典、同代画作并绘制肖像。哈斯克尔将1742–1743年临摹 Van Mour 版画的土耳其题材作品视为他展现奇幻想象力的突出例外；第9章以“奇幻但不严肃”概括其艺术，第17章提及他对 Pietro Longhi 风格的尝试。

**English:** Chapter 10 says Gian Antonio Guardi worked for Marshal Schulenburg from before 1730 until 1745 and received a monthly salary. Haskell says Schulenburg regarded him chiefly as a useful copyist rather than an original painter; Guardi was commissioned only once, in 1737, to paint history pictures of his own, and otherwise copied Venetian masterpieces and contemporary works and produced portraits. Haskell treats the Turkish scenes copied after Van Mour engravings in 1742–1743 as a notable exception that revealed Guardi's imaginative gifts. Chapter 9 characterizes his art as magical but unserious, and Chapter 17 mentions his excursions into Pietro Longhi's manner.

The person main entry is `G.csv#155`, indexed at pp. 258, 312, 315, and 383. Chapters 9 and 10 use the full name Gian Antonio Guardi for the same painter; Chapter 10 note 2 reports Watson's attribution of a series to Gian Antonio rather than Francesco Guardi. This person is distinct from the accepted Francesco Guardi KU in the manifest. Chapter 17 (p. 383) note 1 says only that works were known as Guardi's in Correr's day, without a first name; this source limitation is retained and is not used as an additional identity anchor.
